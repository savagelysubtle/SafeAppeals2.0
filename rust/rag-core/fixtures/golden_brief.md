1. Facts
The claimant filed an appeal on January 1. The board issued a notice of decision regarding the rating reduction proposed in the prior supplemental statement of the case.
Evidence includes medical records spanning three years of treatment history and wage statements from the employer of record documenting reduced industrial capacity across successive rating periods.
Additional supporting narrative describes ongoing functional limitations and vocational impact over multiple rating periods with flare-ups documented by treating physicians in contemporaneous notes.
The claims file further contains lay statements from family members corroborating observed limitations in activities of daily living and intermittent use of assistive devices at home and in the community.
Private treatment notes from the orthopedic clinic describe persistent pain, limited range of motion, and recommended work restrictions that remain in effect despite conservative care.
Diagnostic imaging of the lumbar spine shows multilevel degenerative changes consistent with the reported symptoms and with the functional loss described in the vocational evidence of record.
The VA examination did not adequately account for flare-ups or the combined effect of orthopedic and neurologic findings discussed in the private specialist reports submitted on appeal.

2. Argument
Counsel argues the decision ignored material evidence in the claims file. The record shows ongoing disability supported by treating physician opinions and consistent diagnostic imaging findings.
Additional facts support a remand for further development of the vocational evidence and consideration of combined impairments under the applicable rating schedule for the musculoskeletal system.
The Board failed to address favorable private medical evidence and did not provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases for preferring the VA examiner over the treating specialist.
Where the evidence is at least in equipoise, the claimant is entitled to the benefit of the doubt. The decision should be vacated and the matter remanded for a new examination.
On remand, the Agency should obtain a medical opinion that considers the full longitudinal history, including flare-ups and functional loss described in the lay evidence of record.
The Board also erred in failing to discuss whether extraschedular referral was warranted given the marked interference with employment documented by the employer wage statements.
For these reasons, the appellant respectfully requests that the Board reverse the rating reduction or, in the alternative, remand for further development consistent with this argument.
